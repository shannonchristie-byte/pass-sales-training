An **Access to Higher Education (A2HE) Diploma** is an alternative route onto university degree courses, designed for adults as an alternative to A Levels. Each diploma is tailored to a pathway towards a particular career. It's sold through **Pass Learn**.

- A **full Level 3 qualification**, regulated by the **QAA** (the Quality Assurance Agency, the Access equivalent of Ofqual) through Open Awards, our awarding body.
- **One diploma is academically equivalent to 3 A Levels** in UCAS tariff points. **It's not the same as A Levels.**
- Made up of units, each worth credits. Some units are graded pass, merit or distinction, and some aren't. The credits are worth UCAS points.
- **Fully assignment-based: no exams.**
- There are entry requirements, to make sure accepted learners can complete it.
- **Not useful in Scotland.** The QAA scheme covers England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

## What it isn't

- **It doesn't guarantee university entry.** Universities set their own requirements.
- It isn't a degree. It's a way into one.
- It's widely recognised, but learners should **always check with the university for their specific course**. Highly competitive courses like medicine or dentistry may not accept Access alone, and some Russell Group universities set extra conditions.

## Who it's for

- **16 or over**, a **UK resident**, with **Level 2 maths and English**, or working towards it alongside the diploma.
- People changing career, returning to education after a gap, mature learners, and anyone with no Level 3 qualification (or one that's out of date or irrelevant).
- Motivated by a **specific university course or career**.

If Access doesn't fit, could another Pass service help? Pass A Level, or PFS to get the Level 2 maths and English they need.

## How hard it is

**It's essentially the equivalent of 3 A Levels**, probably done in a much shorter time and with much less guidance than at school. That's around **600 hours of study**. Be honest about this.

## What we can't promise

- University admission is entirely up to the university.
- Buying doesn't guarantee being accepted onto the diploma. There's an application after purchase (see [Eligibility and the application process](#/kb/p?slug=access-eligibility)).

## What to say, and what never to say

| Never say | Say instead |
| --- | --- |
| "This guarantees university entry." | "It's a Level 3 qualification designed as an alternative route into university." |
| "This is the same as A Levels." | "It's equivalent to 3 A Levels in Level 3 study and UCAS points, but it isn't the same as A Levels." |
| "You don't need to check with the university." | "Universities set their own entry requirements, so check your chosen university and course." |
| "You'll definitely be accepted after purchase." | "Buying doesn't guarantee acceptance onto the diploma. Your application still needs reviewing." |
| "You'll definitely be refunded if you change your mind after registration." | "Once you're formally registered with Open Awards, you're no longer entitled to a refund." |
| "It's easy." / "You can do it quickly without much study." | "It's a challenging qualification, needing around 600 hours of study overall." |
| "You get unlimited tutor support." | "Tutor support is there, but you complete the work independently." |
| "You'll definitely get distinctions." | "Each graded unit is marked pass, merit or distinction on the work you submit." |
