## The call at a glance

1. Introduce yourself and confirm the enquiry.
2. Build rapport and understand their goal.
3. Explain what Access to HE is.
4. Confirm which diploma they're interested in.
5. Check eligibility, compatibility, and suitability and commitment.
6. Explain the course, assignments and support.
7. Explain what isn't included and what can't be guaranteed.
8. Explain the application process after purchase.
9. Recommend the right route.
10. Close the sale, confirm the key terms, and ask permission to tick the terms and conditions.
11. Take payment and explain what happens next.

## 1. Introduce yourself and build rapport

> "Hi, is that (name)? My name is (name), and I'm calling from Pass Learn about the Access to Higher Education Diploma you enquired about. Are you still looking at studying an Access to HE course?"

- "What were you hoping to use the Access course for: university, a career change, or something else?"
- "What university course are you hoping this will lead to?"
- "Is there a specific university you want to apply to?"
- "When do you want to start university?"
- "Have you checked whether that university accepts an Access to HE Diploma for that course, or would you like us to check?"

## 2. Reassure them and explain the diploma

> "We can definitely help with that. We've helped hundreds of people in similar situations."

> "You may already know this, but an Access to Higher Education Diploma is a full Level 3 qualification, designed as an alternative route into university for adults. It's academically equivalent to 3 A Levels in Level 3 study and UCAS points. With us it's regulated through the awarding body Open Awards, which is QAA approved, so it's widely recognised by universities. For (their career area), it'll be great."

## 3. Qualify them

> "There are a couple of eligibility points we have to cover before confirming you can take a diploma. Can I run through those now?"

- "Are you currently living in the UK?"
- "Are you aged 16 or over?"
- "Do you have maths and English qualifications, such as GCSEs or Functional Skills Level 2?"

If they don't have Level 2 maths and English:

> "That's fine. We're one of the only providers that let you start your diploma before you've taken maths and English, so you don't need to have passed them to start. You do need to be working towards them during your diploma, because you need to pass them to complete it, and that's something we can help with too."

> "Perfect, based on that you're eligible for our diplomas. I'll explain how they work, and let me know if you have any questions."

See [Eligibility and the application process](#/kb/p?slug=access-eligibility) for the full checks.

## 4. Explain the course

> "You'd have two accounts with us: a learning portal where you do all the learning, and a diploma portal where you submit assignments for marking."

> "Everything's online, so you can study from home around your other commitments. We recommend around 600 hours of study, done flexibly. It's assignment-based, so there are no exams. You complete units and submit assignments as you go."

> "The learning portal breaks the diploma into units, each with in-depth revision slides, practice questions and topic tests, all AI marked with feedback. It tracks your progress, so you can see how far through you are and how you're doing on each unit."

> "You gain credits and UCAS points through marked assignments. Each unit ends with an assignment brief explaining the coursework task. Your work is marked against the assessment criteria, and graded units are graded pass, merit or distinction."

> "You'll have two kinds of tutor: unit tutors for learning support and assignment feedback, and a diploma tutor for tutorials, academic progress, university progression and pastoral support. There are tutorials at key points, you can ask for extra support, and you can submit drafts for feedback where that's available."

> "Once all your assignments are done, your work is quality assured, and your grades go to Open Awards to be confirmed. When you complete successfully, you get your official qualification and certificate."

## 5. Explain the application

> "Access to HE works slightly differently from a normal course purchase. Once you buy, you're sent an application form, completed by you or someone on your behalf. Our admin team then reviews it: ID, ULN where needed, prior qualifications, suitability, and any adjustments or support needs."

> "Buying doesn't automatically guarantee you'll be accepted onto the diploma. Your application still needs to be approved."

- **If rejected:** "If for any reason the application is rejected, the refund policy applies."
- **If approved:** "If approved, you'll be enrolled with Pass Learn, and then we complete your registration with Open Awards."

## 6. Recommend and give the price

> "Based on everything you've told me, the (diploma) Access to HE Diploma sounds like the right route for you. It would give you the Level 3 qualification you need to progress towards (their goal)."

> "You've enquired at a really good time: we have a big sale on. The diplomas are normally £1,500, but there's 20% off at the moment, bringing it down to £1,199. That includes absolutely everything: the full learning platform, the assignment marking, the tutor support and your final grades and certificate, so there's nothing further to pay."

## 7. Close

> "The next step would be to get your purchase done today. Then you'll get your confirmation email and the application form. Our admin team reviews everything before you're fully accepted. Once approved, you move into enrolment and registration and get access to the portal. Are you happy to make a start today?"

## 8. Confirm before payment

> "Just to confirm, this is for the Access to HE Diploma in (diploma). It's a Level 3 qualification designed to support progression to university. It's online and assignment-based, with no exams. The price includes everything: the learning portal, the assignment submissions, both kinds of tutor support, and your final grades and certificate from the QAA-approved awarding body Open Awards, so there's nothing more to pay. Are you happy for me to continue?"

> "Before I take payment, are you happy for me to tick the terms and conditions box on your behalf? You'll get a copy with your order confirmation."

## 9. Take payment and explain what's next

Take payment in full if you can. If they ask about spreading the cost, see [Prices, payment and refunds](#/kb/p?slug=access-prices).

> "Your order's now been placed. You'll also get the application form, which needs completing before your application can be reviewed. Once it's reviewed, we'll update you on next steps. Do you have any final questions? Thank you for your time, and good luck. If you need anything after the call, just get in touch."

## If they hesitate or object

| They say | What to say |
| --- | --- |
| **It's a lot of money** | "I completely understand. It's a big decision, and it's important you feel comfortable. Bear in mind this is a full Level 3 qualification, equivalent to 3 A Levels, designed to support progression into university and long-term career goals. We also have payment options like Klarna, Clearpay and PayPal, which can help spread the cost." |
| **I need to check with the university** | "That's absolutely fine, and it's the right thing to do. I'd rather you check than buy something that doesn't meet your entry requirements. Check the course page or ask admissions whether they accept Access to HE for that course, and whether they need specific grades, units, GCSEs, Functional Skills or work experience." |
| **I'm worried it'll be too hard** | "That's understandable. It's a challenging course, and we don't want to hide that: it's Level 3, equivalent to 3 A Levels. But it's designed for adult learners, with learning materials, assignment guidance, feedback and tutor support. The main thing is whether you can commit the time consistently and stay motivated." |
| **I want to finish very quickly** | "You can study flexibly, but I'd be careful about rushing. There are around 600 hours of learning, and the work needs to be a proper Level 3 standard. To finish quickly, you'd need to be realistic about the hours you can commit each week. Over 24 months that's around 5 to 6 hours a week." |
| **I've been out of education for years** | "That's very common with Access learners. The diplomas are designed for adults, including people returning after a long gap. It may feel challenging at first, which is why it's structured with learning materials, assignments and tutor support." |
| **I'm not very academic** | "That's completely understandable. A lot of learners feel nervous before starting. The key thing is whether you're motivated, willing to put the time in, and ready to work through the materials properly. It's challenging, but designed to be achievable for adults." |
| **Will this get me into university?** | "It can support your application and is widely accepted, but we can't guarantee university entry. The university makes the final decision, so check the entry requirements for your course." |
| **Do I need GCSEs?** | "Universities often ask for maths and English alongside Access. Some accept Functional Skills Level 2, and others ask for GCSEs. It depends on the university and course, so it's important to check. If you need maths or English, we can help with Functional Skills too." |
| **Is it all online?** | "Yes. You access the materials, complete the work and submit assignments through the online portal. There are no exams." |
| **Do I get tutor support?** | "Yes: assignment feedback and tutorials. It's structured support rather than unlimited teaching, so you still work independently, but you're not left on your own." |

**Why Pass Learn:** "It's flexible, online and designed around adult learners. There's no travelling to college or fixed classroom sessions. You access the learning online, submit assignments through the portal and get tutor feedback, with structured tutor support and tutorials for your progress and next steps. For a lot of people working or with family commitments, that flexibility makes it far more realistic than college."
