## What's included

1. Admin approves or rejects each application, then registers successful learners with Open Awards.
2. Our online **learning portal**, with everything needed for the assignments: revision slides, practice questions and topic tests. Topic tests are AI marked, with a mix of short answers and longer pieces of up to 2,000 words. Progress, marks and feedback are all tracked.
3. A **diploma portal**, where they submit assignments and get feedback.
4. Assignments marked and moderated by us, with feedback.
5. Feedback on **draft** assignments from unit tutors.
6. **Tutorials** with their diploma tutor, by phone or video, at set points and on request.
7. At the end, we take their provisional grades to Open Awards to be confirmed.

**Not included:** in-person teaching or face-to-face tuition, or guaranteed entry to the university and course of their choice. It's up to the learner to complete the units. Without the time or motivation, they may not pass.

## Two kinds of tutor

| | Diploma tutor | Unit tutors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | One person for the whole diploma | A qualified specialist for each unit, so it changes by unit |
| For | Pastoral and diploma support: staying on track, freezes, progression planning after the diploma, with a 7-step progression plan | Academic support and assignment feedback |
| Contact | Unlimited messages, scheduled tutorials, and ad hoc tutorials on request | Up to 3 messages per unit through the diploma portal |

Tutorials cover pastoral, academic and university-progression topics. The learner can note points to discuss beforehand, and the tutor writes up a report. They're arranged by email and held by phone or video.

## Study time

**Around 600 hours** in total, as quickly or slowly as they like. We expect people to finish within 24 months, and most finish sooner. Going past 24 months needs an extension, agreed with Open Awards.

| To finish in | Study needed |
| --- | --- |
| 2 years | 5 to 6 hours a week (for someone working 5 days, 2 to 3 hours on each weekend day) |
| 1 year | 10 to 12 hours a week, or 2 hours a day, 6 days a week |
| 6 months | About 23 hours a week, or 4 hours a day, 6 days a week |

## Units and assignments

- All learning material is unlocked from the start, in any order. **Assignment submissions unlock one unit at a time**, so they can't submit the wrong one.
- Each unit has one **assignment brief**: a coursework task to finish by the end of the unit, using the latest version when they start it.
- Each unit has a time limit set by its credits: **30 days for a 3-credit unit, 60 days for a 6-credit unit**.
- **A late submission is capped at a pass.**
- **Two formal submissions** per unit. A third is only possible in exceptional circumstances, through Open Awards, and is capped at a pass.
- **Drafts:** they can submit a draft for general feedback for the first 5 assignments, and for later ones on request.
- Every submission is checked for plagiarism and AI use before marking.
- They need to pass every unit to get the full qualification. A failed unit shows on their transcript. For some next steps a partial diploma is enough.
- They can **freeze** the diploma for up to 6 months.

## Grades

The grades we give during the course are **provisional**. Once every unit is complete, the work is internally quality assured, and we take the grades to Open Awards' **final award board**, which confirms or adjusts them. There's no overall grade: they get a **transcript** of their grade for each unit, plus their certificate. University offers depend on the UCAS points from those grades.

## UCAS deadlines

| Date | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| 15 October | Early deadline: Oxford or Cambridge (any course), and medicine, dentistry and veterinary courses. Late applications are very rarely considered |
| 14 January | The main "equal consideration" deadline for most courses, at 18:00 UK time. **This is the one that matters for most of our learners** |
| 30 June | The final deadline before Clearing. Applications after 14 January are still accepted, but without guaranteed equal consideration |

Many universities also ask for maths and English alongside Access. Some accept Functional Skills Level 2, and others want GCSEs. Some want work experience too, which we can advise on but not arrange.

## FAQs

- **What does Level 3 mean?** There are 9 qualification levels in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. A GCSE pass is Level 2, and an A Level is Level 3. It's a way to compare how difficult qualifications are.
- **I've been out of education for years.** There's no barrier based on time out. It may feel harder at first, but their diploma tutor helps guide them.
- **Can it be used for apprenticeships?** Some, not all. Degree apprenticeships often ask for a UCAS tariff "or equivalent", which Access can meet. Check each one.
- **Will it help me get a job?** It opens up higher education courses, which improve employability.
