## 1. Introduce yourself and the reason for the call

> "Hi, is that (name)? Hi (name), I'm calling from Pass Learn about the A Level qualifications you enquired about. Is this still something you're looking into?"

## 2. Build rapport and reassure

> "Just so I understand how we can best support you: which subjects do you need? What do you need the A Levels for: university, a job, an Access course? When do you need them completed by?"

> "That sounds great. This is definitely something we can help with. We've helped lots of private candidates in the same situation."

## 3. Explain A Levels and their Ofqual status

- All our A Levels are **Ofqual regulated**, recognised by UK courses, universities and employers, and recognised internationally.
- The grade they get is worth a number of **UCAS points**, which are needed to apply for university.
- There are **no official entry requirements**. A GCSE grade 4/C is recommended, but many people do A Levels without one.

## 4. Advise the best route and explain the process

> "From what we've discussed, the best option is the course and exam, so you have all the revision to prepare and the exam booking sorted for you. Let me talk you through how it works."

- Exams are **once a year, in the summer, in person** at an approved centre.
- Once booked, our exams team finds the nearest centre with availability for their subjects and board, checks they're happy, and secures it. If they book coursework or practicals, we arrange those too.
- The team asks for anything else needed, including evidence for access arrangements.
- Results come out for everyone on the same day, usually the second Thursday in August.

> "Do you have any questions so far, or does that sound like it would work for you?"

## 5. Explain the course

> "The full premium course is online, so you can use it anywhere, 24/7, as quickly or slowly as you like, around your other commitments."

Walk through revise, practice questions, online tests, mock exams for each board, and progress tracking. See [The courses](#/kb/p?slug=alevel-courses).

> "It increases your chances of passing first time. Success rates are higher with our course, and it helps brush up on the areas you were weaker in last time."

## 6. Create urgency: the late fees

> "One really important thing is the exam entry deadline. For summer 2027, the deadline before late fees is the end of January. The exam boards set those fees, and they double, then triple, the cost. We strongly advise booking before then, because there's nothing we can do to reduce them once the deadline passes."

> "We've got a 20% off sale running at the moment, so it's a good time to get booked in. For the subjects you're looking at, it would work out as…" (talk through the price)

## 7. Check for questions and reassure again

> "Is there anything you're unsure about: the exam, the booking process or how anything works? You'll have full support throughout. I'll help with the booking to make sure it's done right, and our exams and aftercare team will help with everything after that."

## 8. Next steps

> "The next step is to secure your exam booking with your preferred exam board and subjects, and make sure we avoid the late fees. Shall we get that sorted for you?"

## 9. Confirm and take payment

> "Just to confirm, this is for your Ofqual-regulated A Level in (subject). Before we go through to payment, are you happy for me to tick that you've understood the terms and conditions? You'll get a full copy with your order confirmation."

- Try to take payment in full without mentioning payment plans, unless they ask or seem worried or hesitant. If not, go down the payment plan route. See [Prices and payment](#/kb/p?slug=alevel-prices).
- Take card details.
- Explain that the order is placed and they'll get the order confirmation and a copy of the terms and conditions. If neither arrives within 15 minutes, they should call back.
- Finish by asking if they have any questions, and wish them luck with their revision and exams. Let them know we're happy to help with anything.
