A Star Equivalency offers GCSE equivalency tests, built for learners going into **teaching** (PGCE, QTLS) and sometimes used for **healthcare** courses. They're useful where Functional Skills Level 2 isn't accepted. Teaching is the one area that usually doesn't accept Functional Skills Level 2.

- Our tests are **accepted by universities, ITTs and SCITTs**. A Star has led equivalency testing since 2002, and our certificates are accepted by the vast majority of teacher training providers and some nursing and midwifery courses.
- We offer **courses and exams**, all online, in **Maths (foundation and higher), English, Biology and Combined Science**.
- Only one other company offers these, and we're far better on price, support and quality.
- **Fast-track results** in 2 working days, instead of up to 10, for an extra fee (see [A Star prices](#/kb/p?slug=astar-prices)).

## Which route into teaching?

Teaching is the most complicated route of any profession. Most teaching enquiries come through A Star, though some come through PFS and MME, so the policy lives here. There are three main routes:

| Route | Who it's for | Best option |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PGCE at university, for QTS** (qualified teacher status) | About 90% of applicants: the standard route | **GCSE equivalency (A Star).** Functional Skills isn't accepted |
| **Teaching apprenticeship** | | **Functional Skills (PFS).** Equivalency isn't usually accepted |
| **QTLS** | Teaching post-16, for example college and university lecturers | **Functional Skills (PFS)** is accepted and the best option |

On the PGCE route:

- **Primary teaching** that needs a science: **Combined Science** is the best route.
- **Secondary teaching:** most people choose **Biology**.
- A lot of people swap from Combined Science to Biology later. Asking about this while booking could prevent it.

Providers much prefer applicants to have the qualification before they make an offer, so getting it early strengthens an application a lot.
